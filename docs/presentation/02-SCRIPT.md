# NWSA Music Hub — Speaking Script
### Follows the slide deck, slide for slide. Change anything.

**How to read this**

- **SAY** — words you can say as written. They're written to be spoken, not read.
- **[DO]** — what you're doing while you say it.
- **[LIVE]** — leave the slides and show the actual app.
- **⚠ FILL IN / VERIFY** — something I could not know. Fix it before you present.
- **[PAUSE]** — stop and take questions. Three of them, on purpose.

Total spoken time ≈ 30 minutes at a normal pace. Cut marks are in the
presenter's outline.

## SLIDE 3 — The problem

**[ON SCREEN]** The same fact, entered four times, still wrong somewhere.

> **SAY:** Here's the problem, and it isn't that we were disorganized. It's that
> every single fact about this department had to be entered more than once.
>
> A rehearsal moves. Now it has to change on the calendar, in whatever I sent to
> parents, in the thing on the wall — and the students who weren't in the room
> when I said it are just wrong until somebody tells them.
>
> Attendance was the worst of it. Before this, we ran on a Notion workspace —
> that's where the first roster in this app came from. It was fine on a laptop.
> On a phone, standing in front of an ensemble, it could not do the one thing
> attendance needs: fast tapping. So roll got written on paper and typed up
> later, or it didn't get typed up at all.
>
> ⚠ **FILL IN — this is the most important paragraph in the talk, and it has to
> be yours.** Two or three sentences about what your actual week looked like.
> The evening of texts. The parent who drove for a cancelled rehearsal. The
> stack of paper roll sheets. First person, specific, thirty seconds.
>
> Everything in this tool exists to stop one of those.

## SLIDE 5 — The rule

**[ON SCREEN]** **You set it once. Everyone sees it.**

> **SAY:** If you remember one sentence from today, this is it.
>
> You set it once. Everyone sees it.
>
> When you cancel a rehearsal in the app, you're not also posting it somewhere.
> The cancellation *is* the notice. It turns into a red banner on the front page
> of the site, it changes on every affected student's schedule, and it changes
> in the calendar on the phones of everyone who subscribed.
>
> One action. You don't chase it afterward.
>
> **[DO]** Say it a second time, slower. Let it sit for a beat.

## SLIDE 8 — Take roll: mark only the exceptions

**[ON SCREEN]** Absent · Late · Excused · Lesson — and nothing else.

> **SAY:** This is the part you'll use every single day, so I'm going to do it
> live, on my phone, the way I actually do it.
>
> **[LIVE — Demo C. Hold the phone up. Do not narrate the taps — do them.]**
>
> Here's the ensemble. Everyone on this list is present. I don't touch them.
>
> **[DO]** Tap Absent on one. Tap Late on another.
>
> I only mark the exceptions. Absent. Late. Excused. And Lesson — that one's
> different, watch.
>
> **[DO]** Tap Lesson on a third student.
>
> A lesson isn't an absence. The student steps out for a window of time and
> comes back. So it asks me when, and it makes me give a reason — that field is
> required on purpose. Now that pull-out is documented: it shows on this card,
> it shows on Who's Out, and nobody has to remember why that chair was empty.
>
> **[DO]** Point at the top strip.
>
> Up here are the counts, and each one is a filter — tap "Absent" and it shows
> me just those.
>
> **[DO]** Tap Finish roll — summary.
>
> When I'm done: who's out, and one tap to call or email home. There's a copy
> button that gives me a clean list to paste wherever I need it.
>
> That's it. That was three taps, because three people were out.
>
> A few things it does quietly while you work: if you mark somebody late, it
> records *how* late. It shows you their last five rehearsals as little dots, so
> a pattern is visible without you looking anything up. If a parent reported an
> absence ahead of time, it's sitting right there on that student's row for you
> to accept. And if every single trumpet is out today, it tells you.

## SLIDE 9 — Why that's faster

**[ON SCREEN]** 80 students. 4 out. **4 taps.**

> **SAY:** Here's the structural reason, and it's just arithmetic.
>
> In most attendance systems, the work grows with the size of your ensemble.
> Eighty students, eighty marks — every day, whether or not anything happened.
>
> Here the work grows with the number of *absences*. Eighty students and four
> people out is four taps. Nobody out is zero taps.
>
> ⚠ **VERIFY:** use your real roster number if you have it on screen. Otherwise
> say "an ensemble of about eighty."
>
> I want to be straight with you: I have not timed the old way against the new
> way, so I'm not going to stand here and claim a number of minutes. What I can
> tell you is what changed — the work stopped scaling with the size of the room.
>
> And because it's finished in the room, it's finished. There's no evening
> where you type up the paper.

## SLIDE 10 — Roll answers questions later

**[ON SCREEN]** Receipt · Pattern · Follow-up · Export

> **SAY:** Four things that come out of roll for free.
>
> A **receipt** — the event shows when roll was taken, and by whom. If somebody
> asks whether attendance got done for Tuesday, you look, you don't reconstruct.
>
> A **pattern** — five dots per student, right on the roll screen.
>
> A **follow-up list** — unexcused absences from the past week collect in one
> place, and you clear each one with Excuse, Contacted, or Dismiss. It's a queue
> that's supposed to reach empty.
>
> And an **export** — the attendance you want, as a spreadsheet file, whenever
> someone needs it in writing.

## SLIDE 11 — When the day changes

**[ON SCREEN]** Swap · Shift · Move · Cancel — and undo.

> **SAY:** Things change. That used to be the expensive part of the week. Let
> me show you, not tell you.
>
> **[LIVE]** Pick a real upcoming rehearsal. Open **Schedule Change**, shift its
> time by fifteen minutes, save.
>
> **[DO]** Switch to the public site in a second tab or window. Point: the red
> banner is already there. You didn't post it anywhere — the change IS the
> notice.
>
> That's what happens automatically on every schedule change, whether you swap
> two blocks, shift a time, move a room, or cancel outright: the event gets
> tagged as changed, the red banner appears on the public front page, and
> you're offered a notice that goes out as an urgent announcement.
>
> **[LIVE]** Now tap **Revert to normal**. The app took a snapshot of the
> original before your first change, so this puts back exactly what was there
> and pulls the notice down with it. You are never stuck having damaged the
> calendar.
>
> There's also a **Close a day** button for the whole-school situation — the
> hurricane day. One button cancels everything on that date and posts one
> notice, instead of you editing fourteen events. I'll describe that one rather
> than demo it, in the interest of time.
>
> ⚠ **VERIFY:** only say "hurricane day" if that's the local phrasing. Otherwise
> "the day school closes."

## SLIDE 12 — Subs and pull-outs

**[ON SCREEN]** Temporary. With a reason. Your roster untouched.

> **SAY:** Every one of you borrows students. A trumpet plays with jazz for one
> concert. A singer gets pulled for a lesson every Thursday for a month.
>
> The old way to handle that is to edit the roster and remember to put it back.
> That's how rosters rot. Let me show you the new way.
>
> **[LIVE]** Pull a real student out for one rehearsal, right now. Add the
> student, pick the event, type a reason — it's required — save. That took
> seconds.
>
> **[DO]** Point at the three places that just changed: the "Sub" badge on the
> roll screen, the student now listed on Who's Out for the ensemble they left,
> and — switch to the public tab — the change on their own public schedule.
>
> Your permanent roster was never touched, and the change expires by itself.
> If you're moving a student from one ensemble into another, one entry does
> both sides.
>
> Nobody has to remember to undo anything.

## SLIDE 14 — Repertoire becomes the program

**[ON SCREEN]** Enter the piece once → the program prints itself.

> **SAY:** This one is my favorite, because it turns a chore into a by-product.
>
> You enter a piece once. The full formal title, composer with dates, arranger,
> catalog number, duration, the movements, program notes, a link to a recording,
> a link to the score — and a sheet-music link **per instrument**.
>
> Then you attach that piece to a concert, and put the pieces in the order you
> want them played.
>
> **[LIVE — Demo D]**
>
> That's the printed program. You didn't write it — it was assembled from what
> you already entered, in your order, with the movements and the total runtime.
> The masthead says New World School of the Arts, and I want to say out loud that

>
> The same entry does something else. A student who's saved their name gets a
> "My part" button on that piece — matched to their instrument. Not the folder,
> not all thirty parts. Theirs.
>
> And a piece can belong to more than one ensemble, so a work that wind ensemble
> and choir both perform is one entry, not two.

## SLIDE 17 — Who can see what

**[ON SCREEN]** Public | Staff only

> **SAY:** ⚠ *[Dean]*, this one's mainly for you, and I want to be precise
> rather than reassuring.
>
> **Public, on purpose:** schedules, concerts, repertoire, announcements — and
> a student's name, instrument, section, and which ensemble they're in. That's
> the whole public list. It's public *because* the site lets a student find
> their own schedule without an account, and to do that it has to show them a
> list of names. That's a real trade, and I want it on the record.
>
> **Staff only, always:** grade level, and pronunciation guides — those never
> made it into that trade and never appear publicly. Plus contact details for
> students and guardians, attendance records, progress notes, and private
> lesson schedules. Signed-in staff on the list. Nobody else, ever.
>
> The line is enforced twice: which records the public site is even allowed to
> query, and separately, by name, which fields the answer is allowed to
> contain. Grade doesn't cross it in either direction — even a mistake in the
> app can't leak it, because the second check happens at the database, not in
> the app's code.
>
> If you ever want the public list narrower still, that's a conversation we
> can have — it would mean giving students accounts, and I'd rather you make
> that call knowing the cost.
>
> And announcements are public by design, so nothing private goes in one.

## SLIDE 18 — Who can do what

**[ON SCREEN]** Owner · Director · Teacher · Personnel Assistant

> **SAY:** Four levels of access.
>
> **Owner** — one account. The only one that can add or remove people. ⚠ *That's
> me today.*

## SLIDE 20 — Old way → new way

**[ON SCREEN]** The comparison table.

> **SAY:** So — why is this less work? Not because the screens are nicer.
>
> **[DO]** Walk the table, but only three rows. Pick the three that matter most
> to this room. Don't read all seven.
>
> Attendance used to be written down and typed up. Now it's tapped once, in the
> room, and it's done.
>
> "Is rehearsal cancelled" used to be an evening of individual replies. Now it's
> one edit and a banner.
>
> The concert program used to be a separate document somebody retyped from the
> repertoire list. Now it's generated from the repertoire list.
>
> Here's the whole argument in one line: **every fact gets entered once, by the
> person who knows it, and stops being re-asked.** That's the efficiency. Not the
> app — the arrangement.
>
> And the honest version of that claim: this doesn't make the work disappear. It
> moves it to the front. Somebody has to put the calendar and the roster in
> correctly at the start of a term. What you get back is that you're not
> re-entering it for the next ten months.
