What changed in this round

The three things you asked for, pushed as far as they go. Every colour here has been composited and measured, so the glass is this thin because it is safe to be, not because nobody checked.

More transparentFour times thinner

The panels went from 56% opaque to 14%. What made that possible was turning the background wash upside down: instead of a dark tint over a pale page, the page is dropped a shade and the colours floating behind the glass are all brighter than it. Brightening can only ever help dark text, so the old limit disappeared.

Softer edgesThe glass runs out

The hairline is gone, and so is the edge itself: over the last twenty-two pixels the panel's own frosting fades away, so the blur dissolves rather than stopping at a line. Add a soft glow inside the top edge and a wide bloom underneath, and there is nothing left to call a boundary. Use the Edges control to compare: Crisp is what you saw last time.

LiquidThe panels reform, and the glass stays real

The wave and the oval are gone. Both were a photograph of one page being revealed by a photograph of another, which is why neither could look like a morph: you were watching a picture of a card being stretched. Now the real card moves. It travels to where the next page needs it, grows or shrinks to fit, and the writing inside fades across while it goes. Because it is the real card and not a picture, the glass keeps working the whole way and picks up whatever colour it happens to be over. They start one after another down the page, because liquid lags, and each one goes a hair past its mark and settles back.

The costSlow has a price

Slow is now 900 milliseconds, and Slowest is a second and a half. Worth knowing once: past about half a second a transition stops feeling like something your tap caused and starts feeling like the app is behind you, and while it runs it swallows taps. Quick is there so you can feel both.
